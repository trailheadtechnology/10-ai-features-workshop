# Great Smoky Mountains National Park — Backcountry Camping and Permit Guide

**Document No.:** GRSM-BC-2025-01
**Effective Date:** February 1, 2025 (revised January 5, 2026)
**Issuing Office:** Backcountry Information Office, Great Smoky Mountains National Park, Gatlinburg, TN

## 1. General Requirement

1.1 A backcountry permit and advance reservation are required for all overnight stays in the backcountry of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, year-round. Camping is permitted only at designated backcountry campsites (numbered 1 through 113) and shelters; dispersed camping is prohibited parkwide.

1.2 Reservations and permits are obtained through the Park's online backcountry permit system up to 30 days in advance. The fee is $8 per person per night, capped at $40 per person per trip. There is no lottery; inventory is continuous first-come.

1.3 The permit must be in the trip leader's possession (printed or on a device with adequate battery) and produced on request.

1.4 Fire rules for backcountry sites appear in GRSM-FIRE-2025-02, *Fire Regulations*, Section 4, and differ from the frontcountry rules in that document.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Campsite"** means a designated, numbered backcountry campsite shown on the official trail map.
- **"Shelter"** means one of the 15 stone or frame shelters, 12 of which lie on the Appalachian Trail corridor.
- **"AT thru-hiker"** means a person holding a valid Appalachian Trail thru-hiker permit under Section 6.
- **"Food storage cable"** means the pulley-and-cable system installed at every campsite and shelter for suspending food.

## 3. Reservations and Occupancy

3.1 Maximum party size is 8 persons at most campsites and shelters. A limited list of large-group sites accepts up to 12; group limits are enforced by the reservation system.

3.2 Maximum stay is 3 consecutive nights at any campsite, and 1 night at any shelter.

3.3 Tenting adjacent to a shelter is prohibited except by AT thru-hikers when the shelter is full.

3.4 Reservations are site- and date-specific. Itinerary changes must be made through the permit system or by telephone to the Backcountry Information Office before the affected night.

3.5 Occupancy of a campsite without a reservation for that site and night is a citable offense even where space is available.

## 4. Food Storage

4.1 All food, trash, and scented items must be suspended on the provided food storage cables at all times when not in immediate use, or stored in a hard-sided container approved by the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee (accepted parkwide although the Park's bears are black bears).

4.2 Food storage violations are the leading cause of campsite closures. Sites closed for bear activity are posted on the permit system and the backcountry conditions page; reservations at closed sites are cancelled with refund.

## 5. Water and Sanitation

5.1 All water sources must be treated. Springs marked on the trail map are seasonal; several ridge-crest sites (including campsites 13, 26, and the Icewater Spring shelter in late summer) require water carries of up to one mile.

5.2 Human waste must be buried six inches deep at least 100 feet from water, campsites, and trails. Toilet paper must be buried or packed out. Privies at shelters must be used where present.

5.3 Washing with soap in any stream is prohibited.

## 6. Appalachian Trail Thru-Hikers

6.1 Hikers beginning and ending their trip at least 50 miles outside the Park, traveling only on the AT corridor, may obtain the AT thru-hiker permit ($40, valid 38 days) in lieu of site-specific reservations.

6.2 Thru-hikers must stay at shelters, moving in one direction, no more than one night per shelter; when a shelter is full, tenting within sight of the shelter is authorized.

## 7. Horse and Stock Use

7.1 Stock are permitted only on designated horse trails and at designated horse campsites, which include hitch racks. Stock may not be tied to trees or held within 100 feet of water.

7.2 Parties with stock are limited to 8 persons and 8 head of stock. Certified weed-free feed is required; grazing is prohibited.

## 8. Seasonal Considerations

8.1 High-elevation sites (above 4,500 feet, including all AT shelters east of Newfound Gap) receive snow and ice from November through early April; traction devices and zero-degree equipment are advised in winter.

8.2 Stream crossings without bridges, particularly on Hazel Creek, Forney Creek, and Eagle Creek trails, become impassable after heavy rain. The Park does not rate crossings; turn back when water exceeds knee depth.

8.3 Road access to trailheads varies seasonally; Clingmans Dome Road closes December 1 through March 31, and secondary roads (Parson Branch, Heintooga Ridge) close seasonally as posted in GRSM-FAQ-2026-01 and on the Park's road status page.

## 9. Enforcement

Camping outside designated sites, occupying an unreserved site, or violating food storage requirements is punishable under 36 CFR 1.6 and 2.10. Backcountry rangers patrol the AT corridor and high-use drainages regularly.

## 10. Contact

Backcountry Information Office: (865) 436-1297, daily 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Eastern. Online permits: smokiespermits.nps.gov. Site closures and conditions are updated on the permit system continuously.
