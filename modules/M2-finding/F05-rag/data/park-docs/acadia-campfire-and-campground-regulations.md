# Acadia National Park — Campfire and Campground Regulations

**Document No.:** ACAD-CG-2025-02
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Office of the Superintendent, Acadia National Park, Bar Harbor, ME
**Supersedes:** ACAD-CG-2021-04

## 1. Scope

These regulations govern camping and fire use within Acadia National Park, including Blackwoods, Seawall, and Schoodic Woods campgrounds, the Duck Harbor campground on Isle au Haut, and all other Park lands on Mount Desert Island, the Schoodic Peninsula, and outlying islands. They implement 36 CFR 2.10 and 2.13 and the Superintendent's Compendium.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Designated campground site"** means a numbered campsite within Blackwoods, Seawall, Schoodic Woods, or Duck Harbor campgrounds.
- **"Established fireplace"** means the NPS-installed fire ring, grate, or stone fireplace at a designated campground site or designated picnic area.
- **"Backcountry"** means all Park lands outside designated campgrounds, picnic areas, and roads.
- **"Portable stove"** means a manufactured appliance burning pressurized gas or liquid fuel.

## 3. Camping — General

3.1 Overnight camping within Acadia National Park is permitted only at designated campground sites. There is no backcountry, dispersed, or at-large camping anywhere in the Park, in any season. This restriction reflects the Park's small land area, private inholdings, and fragile thin-soil ecosystems.

3.2 All campground sites require reservations through Recreation.gov. Blackwoods and Seawall operate May through mid-October; Schoodic Woods late May through Columbus Day; Duck Harbor by special reservation mid-May through mid-October, with access by mailboat from Stonington.

3.3 Maximum occupancy is six persons per standard site. Maximum stay is 14 nights per calendar year across all Park campgrounds combined.

3.4 Sleeping in vehicles at trailheads, parking areas, carriage road entrances, or along the Park Loop Road is prohibited and enforced year-round.

3.5 Quiet hours are 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. Generator use is limited to 8:00 a.m. through 8:00 p.m. at Schoodic Woods and prohibited entirely at Duck Harbor.

## 4. Fires

4.1 Wood and charcoal fires are permitted only in established fireplaces at designated campground sites and at the designated picnic areas listed in Appendix A (Thompson Island, Pretty Marsh, Frazer Point, and Seawall picnic areas). Fires at any other location in the Park, including beaches, summits, gravel bars, and all backcountry areas, are prohibited at all times.

4.2 Because camping itself is confined to campgrounds under Section 3.1, there is no circumstance in which an overnight fire is lawful outside an established campground fireplace.

4.3 Fires must be attended at all times and fully extinguished with water before the site is vacated or occupants sleep. During posted high fire danger, the Superintendent may suspend all open fires parkwide, including in campgrounds; suspension notices are posted at campground entrances and ranger stations.

4.4 Portable stoves are permitted at campground sites and picnic areas. Stove use elsewhere in the Park (e.g., for day-hike cooking on summits) is permitted only at the designated picnic areas, not on trails or summits.

4.5 Firewood may not be brought into the Park from outside the State of Maine, and out-of-state visitors may not transport firewood into Maine under state quarantine law. Certified heat-treated firewood and firewood purchased locally are permitted. Gathering of dead and down wood within the Park is prohibited; all firewood must be purchased.

## 5. Food Storage and Wildlife

5.1 All food, coolers, and scented items must be stored in a closed vehicle or provided storage when not in use. While Acadia does not host grizzly bears, black bears, raccoons, and gulls routinely raid unsecured food.

5.2 Feeding any wildlife, including gulls at Thunder Hole and Sand Beach, is prohibited under 36 CFR 2.2.

## 6. Campground-Specific Provisions

6.1 Blackwoods: no hookups; one vehicle per site plus one small tent trailer; showers available outside the Park in Otter Creek.

6.2 Seawall: walk-in tent sites (section D) require carrying equipment up to 200 feet from parking.

6.3 Schoodic Woods: the only campground with electric and water hookups; RV limit 45 feet.

6.4 Duck Harbor: five lean-to shelters only; tents must be pitched inside lean-tos; open fires prohibited at Duck Harbor notwithstanding Section 4.1 — portable stoves only, due to the absence of fire suppression resources on Isle au Haut.

## 7. Winter Camping

7.1 Blackwoods Campground remains open for primitive winter camping December 1 through March 31 by free permit obtained at Park Headquarters, walk-in only from the Route 3 gate. Fires are permitted only in the established fireplaces of the designated winter sites; snow does not suspend Section 4.1.

## 8. Relationship to Other Documents

Trail and carriage road conduct is governed by ACAD-CR-2025-05, *Carriage Roads and Trails Use Guide*. Seasonal closures, including the Cadillac Summit Road vehicle reservation system, are published in ACAD-CL-2026-01, *Seasonal Closures and Access Notice*. Plain-language guidance appears in ACAD-FAQ-2026-01.

## 9. Enforcement

Camping or fires outside the locations authorized by these regulations are punishable under 36 CFR 1.6 by fines up to $5,000 and/or six months' imprisonment. Illegal fire scars on granite summits are treated as resource damage with restitution assessed for restoration costs.
