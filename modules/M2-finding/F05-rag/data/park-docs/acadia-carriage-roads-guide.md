# Acadia National Park — Carriage Roads and Trails Use Guide

**Document No.:** ACAD-CR-2025-05
**Effective Date:** June 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Division of Resource and Visitor Protection, Acadia National Park

## 1. Purpose

This guide establishes use rules for the 45-mile carriage road system and the maintained hiking trail network of Acadia National Park. It implements 36 CFR 1.5, 2.16, and 4.30, and the Superintendent's Compendium. Camping and fire rules are outside its scope; see ACAD-CG-2025-02, *Campfire and Campground Regulations*. Seasonal surface closures are announced under ACAD-CL-2026-01.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Carriage road"** means the crushed-stone motor-free road system constructed 1913–1940, within Park boundaries.
- **"Connector"** means a carriage road segment on private land of the Land & Garden Preserve (Little Long Pond area), open to the public subject to the owner's posted rules.
- **"Class 1 e-bike"** means an electric bicycle with pedal-assist only, ceasing assistance at 20 mph.
- **"Mud season closure"** means a temporary closure of carriage roads during spring thaw to prevent surface damage.

## 3. Permitted and Prohibited Uses — Carriage Roads

3.1 Permitted on all Park carriage roads: pedestrians, non-motorized bicycles, Class 1 e-bikes, equestrians (except as posted around Eagle Lake), and, in winter, skiers and snowshoers.

3.2 Prohibited on all carriage roads: motor vehicles (except administrative and emergency), Class 2 and Class 3 e-bikes, motorized scooters and skateboards, and horse-drawn vehicles other than those operated under concession contract from Wildwood Stables.

3.3 On connectors within the Land & Garden Preserve, bicycles (including all e-bikes) are prohibited where posted; Preserve rules control on Preserve land.

3.4 Cyclists must yield to pedestrians and equestrians, announce when passing, and observe the posted 20 mph limit, reduced to 10 mph on descents with limited sight lines as signed.

3.5 Pets are permitted on carriage roads on a leash not exceeding six feet.

## 4. Hiking Trails

4.1 Bicycles are prohibited on all hiking trails.

4.2 Pets are permitted on most hiking trails on a six-foot maximum leash, but are prohibited on ladder trails (Precipice, Beehive, Ladder Trail on Dorr Mountain, Jordan Cliffs, Beech Cliffs) and on Sand Beach and Echo Lake Beach in season.

4.3 Trail structures, including iron rungs and ladders, must be used as installed; bypassing them on vegetation is prohibited.

4.4 The Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, and Valley Cove trails are typically closed March through mid-August for peregrine falcon nesting; exact dates are set annually in ACAD-CL-2026-01, Section 4.

4.5 Summit vegetation on Cadillac, Sargent, and other open summits is protected; travel is restricted to bare rock and marked routes above treeline signage.

## 5. Winter Use

5.1 When snow cover is present, designated carriage road segments are groomed for classic skiing by volunteer partners. Walkers and snowshoers must not travel in set ski tracks on groomed segments.

5.2 Bicycles, including fat-tire bicycles, are prohibited on groomed segments while grooming is in effect, and on all carriage roads during a posted mud season closure.

5.3 The Park Loop Road closes to vehicles December 1 through April 14 (except the Ocean Drive and Jordan Pond access segments) and is available for skiing, walking, and cycling during the closure; ice conditions are not monitored.

## 6. Mud Season and Surface Protection

6.1 Each spring, typically mid-March through late April, carriage roads are closed to all bicycles and equestrians (and to pedestrians where posted) during thaw. Use of soft carriage road surfaces causes rutting requiring costly repair; closure status is updated weekly during the season and posted at all major entrances and in ACAD-CL-2026-01.

6.2 Year-round, travel off the carriage road surface to bypass puddles or gates is prohibited where posted for shoulder revegetation.

## 7. Equestrian Use

7.1 Equestrian use is permitted on carriage roads except the Eagle Lake loop and Witch Hole Pond loop as posted. Riders must remove manure from the traveled surface at gates and intersections.

7.2 Overnight stabling is available only through Wildwood Stables; horses may not be kept overnight at campgrounds.

## 8. Accessibility

8.1 The carriage road system's grades generally do not exceed 8 percent; the Eagle Lake and Jordan Pond loops are the most commonly recommended for wheelchair and adaptive-cycle users. Motorized wheelchairs and OPDMDs used by persons with mobility disabilities are permitted under 36 CFR 1.5 determinations posted at the Hulls Cove Visitor Center.

## 9. Events and Groups

9.1 Organized events (races, rides, or groups exceeding 25 persons) on carriage roads require a special use permit from the Office of the Superintendent, applied for at least four weeks in advance.

9.2 Commercial bicycle touring operates under concession or CUA authorization only; clients must be accompanied by an authorized guide.

## 10. Enforcement and Contact

Violations are punishable under 36 CFR 1.6. Rangers and mounted patrol enforce these rules; repeat e-bike class violations result in revocation of Park cycling privileges under the Compendium. Questions: (207) 288-3338, or the Hulls Cove Visitor Center. Plain-language answers appear in ACAD-FAQ-2026-01.
