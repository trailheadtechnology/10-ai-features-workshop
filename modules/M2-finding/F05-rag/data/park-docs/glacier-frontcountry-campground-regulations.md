# Glacier National Park — Frontcountry Campground Regulations

**Document No.:** GLAC-FC-2025-01
**Effective Date:** April 15, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Division of Visitor Services, Glacier National Park
**Supersedes:** GLAC-FC-2022-03

## 1. Applicability

These regulations govern conduct in the Park's developed frontcountry campgrounds, including Apgar, Fish Creek, Sprague Creek, Avalanche, Many Glacier, Rising Sun, Two Medicine, St. Mary, Bowman Lake, Kintla Lake, and Cut Bank. They do not apply to backcountry campsites or the chalet areas, which are governed by GLAC-BC-2025-04, *Backcountry Camping Guide*. Where these regulations conflict with a provision of the Superintendent's Compendium, the Compendium controls.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Campsite"** means a numbered site within a designated frontcountry campground.
- **"Designated fire ring"** means the metal fire grate or ring installed and maintained by the National Park Service at a campsite or picnic area.
- **"Camping party"** means all persons and vehicles occupying a single campsite under one registration.
- **"Quiet hours"** means the period from 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. daily.

## 3. Registration and Occupancy

3.1 All camping parties must register at the campground kiosk or through Recreation.gov before occupying a site. Reservations are required at Fish Creek, Many Glacier, and St. Mary during the peak season (June 15 through September 10); other campgrounds operate first-come, first-served except as posted.

3.2 A campsite may be occupied by no more than eight persons and two vehicles unless posted otherwise. Group sites at Apgar and Two Medicine accommodate 9 to 24 persons by reservation only.

3.3 Maximum stay is 14 nights parkwide between July 1 and Labor Day, and 30 nights in any calendar year.

3.4 Sites left unoccupied for more than 24 hours are considered vacated and may be re-assigned.

## 4. Campfires

4.1 Campfires are permitted in frontcountry campgrounds only in designated fire rings. Building a fire on bare ground, in a self-supplied fire pan, or in any other location is prohibited.

4.2 Campfire season in frontcountry campgrounds runs from May 15 through September 30 annually. Fires are permitted during this season unless the posted fire danger rating is Very High or Extreme, or unless a temporary fire restriction has been issued under the Superintendent's Compendium, in which case all wood fires are suspended parkwide until the restriction is lifted. Current fire danger ratings are posted at campground kiosks and entrance stations each morning.

4.3 Outside the campfire season (October 1 through May 14), wood fires are prohibited in frontcountry campgrounds. Pressurized-gas stoves may be used year-round.

4.4 Only firewood purchased within Flathead, Glacier, or Pondera counties, or dead and down wood collected outside the Park, may be burned. Cutting standing trees or gathering wood within the Park is prohibited in frontcountry areas.

4.5 Fires must not exceed the confines of the fire ring, must be attended by an adult at all times, and must be extinguished with water until cold to the touch before the party sleeps or leaves the site. Coals and ash must be left in the ring; do not scatter them.

4.6 This section applies to frontcountry campgrounds only. Backcountry fire rules, including the site-level no-wood-fire designations, differ substantially and are set out in GLAC-BC-2025-04, Section 4.

## 5. Food Storage

5.1 Glacier National Park is occupied grizzly bear habitat. All food, coolers, cookware, garbage, pet food, and scented items must be stored in a hard-sided vehicle or in a Park-provided food locker at all times when not in immediate use, day and night.

5.2 Placing any of the items in Section 5.1 in a tent, or leaving them unattended on a picnic table, constitutes a violation of 36 CFR 2.10(d) and may result in citation and eviction from the campground.

5.3 Garbage must be deposited in bear-resistant receptacles provided at each campground loop.

## 6. Vehicles, Pets, and General Conduct

6.1 Vehicles must remain on paved or gravel pads. Vehicle length limits are posted per campground; Sprague Creek does not admit towed units of any length.

6.2 Pets must be leashed (six feet maximum) at all times and are not permitted on trails, including trails departing from campgrounds. Pets may not be left unattended.

6.3 Generators may be operated only between 8:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m., and are prohibited at all times at Sprague Creek, Bowman Lake, and Kintla Lake.

6.4 During quiet hours, audio equipment and loud voices audible beyond the occupied campsite are prohibited.

## 7. Seasonal Operations

Campground opening and closing dates vary by elevation and snowpack and are announced each spring. Avalanche Campground typically operates late June through early September; access in 2026 may additionally be affected by the trail closures described in GLAC-CL-2026-01, *Seasonal Closures and Trail Status*. Winter camping is available at Apgar and St. Mary in designated lots with self-registration; no water or fires are available in winter.

## 8. Enforcement

These regulations are enforced under 36 CFR Parts 1 and 2. Violations may result in fines, eviction, and revocation of camping privileges. Questions may be directed to the campground office at (406) 888-7800.
