# Rocky Mountain National Park — Fire Restrictions and Stove Regulations

**Document No.:** ROMO-FIRE-2025-01
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Office of the Superintendent, Rocky Mountain National Park, Estes Park, CO
**Supersedes:** ROMO-FIRE-2022-02

## 1. Purpose

These regulations govern open fires, charcoal, and stove use within Rocky Mountain National Park, implementing 36 CFR 2.13 and the Superintendent's Compendium. The Park's fire posture reflects the 2020 East Troublesome fire, which burned approximately 30,000 acres within Park boundaries; several provisions below are more restrictive than those of neighboring jurisdictions, and visitors should not assume that rules from adjacent National Forest lands apply inside the Park.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Developed area fire site"** means an NPS-installed fire ring or grate at a frontcountry campground site or at a picnic area listed in Appendix A.
- **"Wilderness campsite"** means a designated backcountry campsite administered under ROMO-WC-2026-01, *Wilderness Camping Guide*.
- **"Open fire"** means any wood, charcoal, or unpressurized-solid-fuel fire, including twig stoves.
- **"Pressurized stove"** means a manufactured appliance burning pressurized gas or liquid fuel with an on/off valve.

## 3. Baseline Rules — Frontcountry

3.1 Open fires are permitted only in developed area fire sites at the following campgrounds: Moraine Park, Glacier Basin, Aspenglen, Timber Creek, and Longs Peak, and at the picnic areas listed in Appendix A (including Sprague Lake, Endovalley, and Hidden Valley).

3.2 Fires must remain within the installed ring or grate, be attended continuously, and be extinguished with water until cold to the touch.

3.3 Only purchased firewood or dead and down wood collected outside the Park may be burned; gathering wood within the Park is prohibited in all areas, frontcountry and wilderness alike. Firewood transported from areas under emerald ash borer quarantine must be certified heat-treated.

## 4. Baseline Rules — Wilderness

4.1 Open fires are prohibited at all wilderness campsites and throughout the Park's designated wilderness, with the sole exception of the specific sites listed in Appendix B as "fire sites," each of which is equipped with a metal fire ring. As of the effective date, fewer than a dozen of the Park's roughly 120 wilderness campsites are Appendix B fire sites, all below 9,500 feet on the east side.

4.2 At all other wilderness campsites, and everywhere above 9,500 feet, open fires are prohibited year-round regardless of fire danger. Pressurized stoves are permitted at all wilderness campsites except during a Stage 3 restriction under Section 5.

4.3 Fires at Appendix B fire sites are suspended automatically whenever the posted fire danger is Very High or Extreme, and during any staged restriction.

## 5. Staged Restrictions

5.1 The Superintendent may declare staged restrictions based on fuel conditions, weather, and regional fire activity:

- **Stage 1:** All open fires prohibited parkwide, including Appendix B fire sites; developed area fire sites permitted for charcoal and wood.
- **Stage 2:** All open fires prohibited parkwide, including developed area fire sites; pressurized stoves permitted.
- **Stage 3:** All ignition sources prohibited except pressurized stoves within developed campgrounds; smoking prohibited except in enclosed vehicles.

5.2 Declarations are posted at entrance stations, wilderness offices, campground kiosks, and the Park website, and are typically coordinated with, but not identical to, restrictions on the surrounding Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests. Where Park and Forest stages differ, the Park's declaration controls within Park boundaries.

5.3 Stage 1 or higher restrictions have been in effect for some portion of every summer since 2018; visitors planning July through September trips should expect wilderness fire prohibitions to be effectively total.

## 6. Prohibited Acts — All Areas, All Times

6.1 Parkwide, at all times: fires outside installed rings and grates; unattended fires; burning garbage or food waste; fireworks; sky lanterns; exploding targets; and the use of any accelerant other than commercial fire starters.

6.2 Smoking is prohibited on all trails and within the wilderness at all times, except at wilderness campsites in an area cleared to mineral soil, and is further restricted during staged restrictions per Section 5.1.

6.3 Operating a charcoal grill anywhere other than a developed area fire site is prohibited, including in parking areas and on the Trail Ridge Road corridor.

## 7. Elevation and Alpine Considerations

7.1 Approximately one-third of the Park lies above treeline. In alpine tundra, all open flame, including pressurized stoves, must be operated on bare rock or gravel; operating any flame on tundra vegetation is prohibited and treated as resource damage.

7.2 High winds along the Continental Divide can carry embers substantial distances; the Superintendent may impose localized fire suspensions in wind events without a full staged declaration.

## 8. Liability and Enforcement

Violations are punishable under 36 CFR 1.6 and 2.13 by fines up to $5,000 and six months' imprisonment. Persons whose fires escape are liable for suppression costs; following East Troublesome, the Park refers all escaped-fire cases for prosecution.
