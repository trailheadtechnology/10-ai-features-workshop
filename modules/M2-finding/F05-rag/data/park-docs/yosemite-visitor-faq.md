# Yosemite National Park — Frequently Asked Questions

**Document No.:** YOSE-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation, Yosemite National Park

This document is a plain-language summary for the 2026 season. It does not replace the regulations it summarizes; where it conflicts with YOSE-FIRE-2025-02, YOSE-WILD-2026-01, or the Superintendent's Compendium, those documents control.

## 1. Getting In

**Q: Do I need a reservation to drive into Yosemite in 2026?**
A: Yes, on peak dates. A vehicle reservation is required to enter between 6:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. on weekends and holidays from May 23 through June 14, daily from June 15 through August 16, and weekends through October 12, 2026. Visitors with lodging, campground, or wilderness permit confirmations are exempt for the dates of their stay. Reservations are separate from the entrance fee.

**Q: Is Tioga Road open?**
A: Tioga Road (Highway 120 through the park) typically opens between late May and early July and closes with the first major snowfall, usually November. Glacier Point Road usually opens in May. Check the Park's road conditions line at (209) 372-0200 for current status; closures are documented in YOSE-CL-2026-01.

## 2. Camping

**Q: How do I get a campsite in Yosemite Valley?**
A: All Valley campgrounds are reservation-only. Reservations open on Recreation.gov five months in advance in one-month blocks at 7:00 a.m. Pacific Time and typically sell out within minutes. Cancellations are re-released continuously, so checking repeatedly is worthwhile.

**Q: Can I sleep in my car?**
A: Only within a numbered campsite you are registered to. Sleeping in vehicles at pullouts, trailhead lots, or along roads is prohibited.

## 3. Campfires

**Q: Can I have a campfire at my campsite?**
A: In developed campgrounds, yes, in the installed fire ring, but note the Valley floor hours: from May 1 through September 30, fires on the Valley floor are allowed only between 5:00 p.m. and 10:00 p.m. Campgrounds outside the Valley have no hour restriction unless a temporary fire restriction is in effect. See YOSE-FIRE-2025-02, Section 3.

**Q: Can I have a campfire while backpacking?**
A: Only below 9,600 feet elevation, and only in an already-established fire ring. Above 9,600 feet, campfires are prohibited year-round everywhere in the Park. Gas stoves are fine at any elevation outside the most severe restriction stage. During dry periods the Superintendent may ban wilderness fires entirely, so check the current restriction stage when you pick up your permit. See YOSE-FIRE-2025-02, Sections 4 through 6.

**Q: Do I need any kind of fire permit?**
A: A California Campfire Permit is required for any stove or fire in wilderness. It is included automatically with your Yosemite wilderness permit.

## 4. Wilderness Trips

**Q: How far in advance do I need a wilderness permit?**
A: Reservations open by lottery 24 weeks before your entry date and popular trailheads (Happy Isles, Cathedral Lakes, Lyell Canyon) fill immediately. Forty percent of each trailhead's quota is held back for in-person issuance starting at 11:00 a.m. the day before entry. See YOSE-WILD-2026-01, Section 2.

**Q: Do I really need a bear canister?**
A: Yes. An approved bear-resistant container is required for all overnight wilderness stays, everywhere in the Park, in every season. Hanging food is not an accepted alternative in Yosemite. Rentals are available at wilderness centers.

**Q: How do I climb Half Dome?**
A: The cables are typically up from the Friday before Memorial Day through the second Monday of October. Day hikers need a Half Dome permit from the preseason lottery (March) or the two-day-in-advance daily lottery. Backpackers request a Half Dome rider with their wilderness permit.

## 5. Wildlife and Safety

**Q: Are there grizzly bears in Yosemite?**
A: No. Yosemite has only American black bears. They are, however, highly skilled at obtaining human food. Never leave food in your vehicle overnight in the Valley; use the food lockers at trailheads and campsites. Improper food storage is a citable offense.

**Q: Is the water safe to drink?**
A: Tap water in developed areas is potable. All lake, river, and stream water should be treated before drinking.

**Q: Are drones allowed?**
A: No. Operating unmanned aircraft is prohibited throughout the Park.

## 6. Miscellaneous

**Q: Where can I see giant sequoias?**
A: Mariposa Grove (largest, served by shuttle from the Welcome Plaza), Tuolumne Grove, and Merced Grove. Overnight camping is prohibited in all groves, and fires are prohibited within grove boundaries at all times.

**Q: Can I swim in the Merced River?**
A: Yes, in most reaches when conditions are safe, except within 100 feet of designated riparian restoration zones as posted. Swimming is prohibited above Vernal and Nevada Falls and in Hetch Hetchy Reservoir.

**Q: What if I have questions this document does not answer?**
A: Call (209) 372-0200 or ask at any visitor center. Wilderness-specific questions: (209) 372-0740.
