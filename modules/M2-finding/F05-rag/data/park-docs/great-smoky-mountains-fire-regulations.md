# Great Smoky Mountains National Park — Fire Regulations

**Document No.:** GRSM-FIRE-2025-02
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Office of the Superintendent, Great Smoky Mountains National Park
**Supersedes:** GRSM-FIRE-2019-01

## 1. Purpose

These regulations govern all open fires, charcoal fires, and stove use within Great Smoky Mountains National Park, implementing 36 CFR 2.13 and the Superintendent's Compendium. Following the November 2016 Chimney Tops 2 fire, fire management within the Park is subject to heightened enforcement; visitors should treat every provision of this document as strictly enforced.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Established fire ring"** means an NPS-installed steel ring or grate at a frontcountry campground or picnic pavilion, or the constructed fire ring maintained at a designated backcountry campsite or shelter.
- **"Heat-treated firewood"** means firewood bundled and certified by the USDA or a state agency as heat-treated to kill pests.
- **"Fire danger rating"** means the rating (Low through Extreme) posted daily at visitor centers and campground kiosks.
- **"Burn ban"** means a temporary prohibition on some or all fire types issued under Section 6.

## 3. Frontcountry Fires

3.1 Fires in frontcountry campgrounds (including Cades Cove, Elkmont, Smokemont, Deep Creek, Cosby, and Balsam Mountain) and picnic areas are permitted only in established fire rings and grates, at any hour, unless a burn ban is in effect.

3.2 Only heat-treated certified firewood may be brought into the Park. Untreated firewood may not cross the Park boundary regardless of origin, including from adjacent counties. Dead and down wood may be collected within the Park for immediate use in frontcountry campgrounds.

3.3 Fires must be attended at all times and extinguished with water until cold before occupants sleep or vacate the site.

## 4. Backcountry Fires

4.1 Fires at designated backcountry campsites and shelters are permitted only in the established fire ring at each site, using only dead and down wood collected in the vicinity, unless a burn ban is in effect.

4.2 Fires are prohibited at all times at any location other than an established ring, including along trails, at stream sides, and at undesignated clearings.

4.3 At shelters, fires are permitted only in the exterior fire ring; fires inside shelters, including in historic interior fireplaces, are prohibited.

4.4 Backcountry fires must be no larger than necessary for cooking and warmth, must remain within the ring, and must be extinguished cold before departure or sleep. Half-burned logs may not be left in rings.

4.5 Camping itself is confined to designated sites under GRSM-BC-2025-01, *Backcountry Camping and Permit Guide*; there is accordingly no lawful fire location in the backcountry other than the rings at those sites.

## 5. Stoves

5.1 Pressurized-gas and liquid-fuel stoves are permitted parkwide, including during Stage 1 burn bans.

5.2 Alcohol stoves, tablet stoves, and twig-burning stoves are treated as open fires under these regulations and are prohibited wherever and whenever open fires are prohibited.

## 6. Burn Bans

6.1 The Superintendent may impose staged restrictions based on the fire danger rating, drought indices, and staffing:

- **Stage 1:** All backcountry fires prohibited; frontcountry fires permitted in established rings.
- **Stage 2:** All open fires prohibited parkwide, including frontcountry campgrounds and charcoal grills; pressurized-fuel stoves permitted.

6.2 Burn bans take effect upon posting at visitor centers, campground kiosks, and the Park website, and supersede Sections 3 and 4 while in effect. Autumn (October–November) is the historical peak of fire danger in this Park, contrary to the summer peak typical of western parks.

6.3 Smoking is prohibited while traveling on trails during any burn ban, and at all times within 25 feet of any structure.

## 7. Prohibited Acts — All Areas, All Times

7.1 The following are prohibited parkwide regardless of fire danger: fires outside established rings; use of accelerants other than commercial fire starters; burning of garbage, food waste, treated lumber, or synthetic materials; fireworks and sky lanterns; and abandoning a fire before it is cold to the touch.

7.2 Cutting or breaking limbs from standing trees, living or dead, is prohibited.

## 8. Liability

Persons responsible for a fire that escapes, whether or not the underlying fire was lawful, are liable for suppression costs and resource damage in addition to criminal penalties under 36 CFR 1.6 and 2.13. Where a violation of these regulations results in a wildfire, the matter is referred for felony prosecution where statutes permit.
