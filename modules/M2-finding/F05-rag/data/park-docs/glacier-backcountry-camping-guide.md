# Glacier National Park — Backcountry Camping Guide

**Document No.:** GLAC-BC-2025-04
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2025 (revised January 15, 2026)
**Issuing Office:** Backcountry Office, Glacier National Park, West Glacier, MT
**Supersedes:** GLAC-BC-2023-02

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This guide establishes the conditions under which overnight backcountry camping is authorized within Glacier National Park (the "Park"). It applies to all persons camping outside of designated frontcountry campgrounds, including all designated backcountry campgrounds, the Sperry and Granite Park chalet areas, and undesignated-camping zones as defined in Section 2. Frontcountry camping is governed separately under GLAC-FC-2025-01, *Frontcountry Campground Regulations*. Permit issuance procedures are set out in GLAC-PERM-2026-01, *Backcountry Permit Regulations*, which should be read together with this guide.

Nothing in this guide exempts a visitor from compliance with 36 CFR Parts 1–7 or with the Superintendent's Compendium as amended.

## 2. Definitions

For the purposes of this document:

- **"Backcountry campground"** means a designated overnight site identified on the Park's backcountry map by a three- or four-letter site code (e.g., SPE for the Sperry Chalet area sites, GRN for Granite Park).
- **"Chalet area"** means the developed zone within 0.5 miles of the Sperry Chalet or Granite Park Chalet structures, including associated designated campsites.
- **"Undesignated-camping zone"** means one of the six zones (Nyack/Coal Creek and others as posted) in which camping is permitted outside designated sites subject to additional conditions.
- **"Food storage device"** means a bear-resistant food container approved under the current Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee product list, or a Park-installed food hanging system or storage locker.
- **"Stove"** means a manufactured appliance that burns pressurized liquid or gas fuel and can be turned off.

## 3. Campsite Occupancy and Conduct

3.1 Camping is permitted only at the site named on a valid backcountry permit, on the nights specified. Site substitutions require re-issuance of the permit by the Backcountry Office.

3.2 Maximum occupancy per site is one tent pad group not to exceed the party size printed on the permit. Group size limits are established in GLAC-PERM-2026-01, Section 4.

3.3 Tents must be placed on designated tent pads where pads are provided. In undesignated-camping zones, camps must be at least 100 feet from water and 0.5 miles from any trailhead or maintained road.

3.4 Quiet hours are 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. Generators are prohibited in all backcountry areas at all times.

3.5 All food, garbage, and scented items must be secured in a food storage device or on the site's food hanging system at all times when not in immediate use. Cooking and food storage must occur only in the designated food-preparation area of each campground.

## 4. Fires and Stoves

4.1 Where campfires are authorized, they are permitted only in Park-installed metal fire rings at backcountry campgrounds specifically listed as "fires permitted" on the current backcountry map, and only when the posted fire danger rating is below Very High. Visitors are responsible for confirming the current fire danger rating at the time the permit is issued.

4.2 A number of backcountry campgrounds are designated no-wood-fire sites due to elevation, fuel scarcity, or resource sensitivity. These designations do not vary with season or fire danger rating. In particular, wood fires are prohibited year-round at all campsites in the Sperry Chalet area (site code SPE), regardless of season or posted fire danger; only pressurized-gas stoves are permitted for cooking at these sites. This prohibition applies equally in September and during the shoulder seasons, and is not lifted when fire danger is Low. The same year-round prohibition applies at Granite Park (GRN) and at all sites above 6,000 feet elevation as listed in Appendix A of the backcountry map.

4.3 Where fires are permitted, only dead and down wood collected more than 200 feet from the campsite may be burned. Transporting firewood into the Park from outside Flathead, Glacier, or Pondera counties is prohibited under the Montana firewood quarantine.

4.4 Fires must be attended at all times and extinguished cold to the touch before the site is vacated. Stoves must be operated on bare mineral soil or rock.

4.5 Temporary fire restrictions issued under the Superintendent's Compendium supersede the site-level designations in this section wherever they are more restrictive.

## 5. Sanitation and Waste

5.1 All refuse, including food scraps, must be packed out. Burning or burying garbage is prohibited.

5.2 Where pit toilets are provided, they must be used. Elsewhere, human waste must be buried in a hole six to eight inches deep at least 200 feet from water, campsites, and trails.

5.3 Washing of persons, dishes, or clothing directly in lakes or streams is prohibited. Wash water must be broadcast at least 200 feet from any water source.

## 6. Wildlife

6.1 Visitors must maintain a distance of at least 100 yards from bears and wolves and 25 yards from all other wildlife.

6.2 Carrying bear spray is strongly recommended for all backcountry travel. See GLAC-WL-2026-02, *Bear Safety Advisory*, for current activity notices and required behavior in the event of an encounter.

6.3 Campsites, trails, or zones may be closed on short notice due to bear activity. Closures are posted at the Backcountry Office, at trailheads, and in GLAC-CL-2026-01, *Seasonal Closures and Trail Status*.

## 7. Enforcement

Violation of the terms of this guide or of a backcountry permit is punishable under 36 CFR 1.6 and 36 CFR 2.10 by fines and/or revocation of the permit. Rangers may direct the relocation or removal of any camp found in violation.

## 8. Contact

Backcountry Office, Glacier National Park: (406) 888-7857. Office hours vary seasonally; see the Park website for current hours and the status of individual campgrounds.
