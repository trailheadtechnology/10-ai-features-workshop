# Rocky Mountain National Park — Timed Entry and Visitor FAQ, 2026

**Document No.:** ROMO-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation, Rocky Mountain National Park

This document answers common questions about the 2026 timed entry pilot and general visitation. It is informational; the Superintendent's Compendium and the regulations cited control where any conflict exists.

## 1. Timed Entry Basics

**Q: Do I need a timed entry reservation in 2026?**
A: Yes, for most daytime entries between May 22 and October 19, 2026. Two reservation types exist:

1. **Park Access+ (Bear Lake Road Corridor):** required 5:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Covers the Bear Lake Road corridor and the rest of the Park.
2. **Park Access (rest of Park):** required 9:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. Covers everything except the Bear Lake Road corridor, including Trail Ridge Road, the Wild Basin and Longs Peak areas, and the west side.

Entering outside the stated windows requires no reservation. An entrance pass is required in all cases and is separate.

**Q: When are reservations released?**
A: On Recreation.gov at 8:00 a.m. Mountain Time on the first day of the preceding month (June reservations released May 1, and so on), with approximately 40 percent of inventory held back and released at 7:00 p.m. the night before each entry date.

**Q: How long is my reservation valid?**
A: It admits one vehicle within a two-hour entry window. Once inside, you may remain for the rest of the day and re-enter that same day with your receipt.

## 2. Exceptions and Edge Cases

**Q: I have a wilderness camping permit. Do I need a timed entry reservation?**
A: No, for the day your permit begins. A wilderness permit issued under ROMO-WC-2026-01 serves as your entry authorization for the trip's start date, at any entrance, at any hour. Display the permit on your dashboard at the authorized trailhead.

**Q: I have a campground reservation inside the Park.**
A: Your campground confirmation admits you on check-in day without a separate timed entry reservation.

**Q: What about entering before 5:00 a.m. to climb Longs Peak?**
A: Entries before 5:00 a.m. require no reservation, and most Keyhole Route parties start between 2:00 and 4:00 a.m. for lightning-safety reasons in any case. Note that the non-technical season on the Keyhole Route is short; see ROMO-WC-2026-01, Section 7.3.

**Q: Can I ride a bicycle or walk in without a reservation?**
A: Yes. Timed entry applies to motor vehicles only. Cyclists on Trail Ridge Road should carry layers; the road crosses 12,183 feet and weather changes rapidly.

## 3. Roads and Seasons

**Q: When does Trail Ridge Road open?**
A: Typically by Memorial Day weekend, weather permitting, closing for the season in mid-October after the first sustained storms. In 2026, the road opened fully on May 23. Temporary day closures for snow occur in every month; call the recorded status line at (970) 586-1222.

**Q: Is Old Fall River Road open?**
A: The one-way uphill historic route typically opens July 1 through late September, 2026 dates conditions permitting. Vehicles over 25 feet and all trailers are prohibited.

## 4. Camping and Fires

**Q: How do I camp overnight in the backcountry?**
A: Reserve a designated wilderness campsite (season inventory opened March 2, 2026, on Recreation.gov; $36 per trip). Party limit is 7 at standard sites. A bear-resistant container is required May through October. See ROMO-WC-2026-01, *Wilderness Camping Guide*.

**Q: Can I have a campfire in the backcountry?**
A: Almost certainly not. Open fires are prohibited at all but a small list of designated east-side wilderness fire sites below 9,500 feet, and even those are suspended when fire danger is Very High or during the staged restrictions that have applied in some part of every recent summer. Above 9,500 feet the prohibition is year-round and unconditional. Pressurized-gas stoves are permitted at all sites outside a Stage 3 restriction. See ROMO-FIRE-2025-01, Section 4.

**Q: Where can I have a fire in the frontcountry?**
A: In the installed rings at the five drive-in campgrounds and listed picnic areas, unless a staged restriction is posted. Wood gathering inside the Park is prohibited everywhere; bring certified or locally purchased wood.

## 5. Safety and Wildlife

**Q: What is the biggest hazard in the Park?**
A: Afternoon lightning above treeline, July through early September. Plan to be below treeline by noon on Trail Ridge Road area trails and summit routes.

**Q: How close can I get to elk and moose?**
A: Stay 25 yards from elk and moose (75 yards during the autumn elk rut, when meadow closures are posted in Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park). Moose in the Kawuneeche Valley injure visitors more often than any other species on the west side.

**Q: Is there cell coverage?**
A: Intermittent on the east side, largely absent on the west side and in wilderness. Do not rely on a phone for emergencies.
