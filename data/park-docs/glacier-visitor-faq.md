# Glacier National Park — Frequently Asked Questions

**Document No.:** GLAC-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation and Education, Glacier National Park

This document answers common visitor questions for the 2026 season. It is informational only; where it conflicts with a regulation or the Superintendent's Compendium, the regulation controls. Cross-referenced documents are available at visitor centers and on the Park website.

## 1. Entry and Reservations

**Q: Do I need a vehicle reservation to enter the Park in 2026?**
A: Yes, for some corridors. Between June 13 and September 28, 2026, a timed vehicle reservation is required to enter the Going-to-the-Sun Road corridor from the west side and the Many Glacier corridor between 6:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. Reservations are released on Recreation.gov 120 days in advance, with a next-day block released at 7:00 p.m. Mountain Time. Entry before 6:00 a.m. or after 3:00 p.m. does not require a reservation. A park entrance pass is required in all cases and is separate from the reservation.

**Q: Is the Going-to-the-Sun Road open?**
A: The alpine section opened June 27, 2026. Typical full-road season is late June or early July through the third week of October, weather depending. See GLAC-CL-2026-01, *Seasonal Closures and Trail Status*, Section 3, for current road status.

## 2. Trails and Conditions

**Q: Can I hike to Avalanche Lake?**
A: Not at present. The Avalanche Lake Trail is closed effective June 20, 2026, until further notice, because the footbridge over Avalanche Creek washed out in mid-June 2026. The Trail of the Cedars loop remains open. See GLAC-CL-2026-01, Section 4.1. No reopening date has been announced.

**Q: Is Hidden Meadow Trail open?**
A: It is open with restrictions. Because of grizzly bear activity reported in late June 2026, hikers must travel in groups of four or more and off-trail travel near the meadow is prohibited. See GLAC-WL-2026-02, *Bear Safety Advisory*, Section 5.1.

**Q: When is the snow gone from high trails?**
A: Passes typically melt out between mid-July and early August. Gunsight Pass and Swiftcurrent Pass hold snow the longest. Check trail status boards at visitor centers.

## 3. Camping and Permits

**Q: How do I get a backcountry permit for 2026?**
A: Advance reservations were allocated by lottery, with applications accepted March 1–15, 2026, on Recreation.gov. If you missed the lottery, unclaimed inventory went on sale April 15, and walk-up permits are issued in person starting 24 hours before your trip at the Apgar Backcountry Permit Center and other listed stations. Maximum party size is eight. See GLAC-PERM-2026-01, *Backcountry Permit Regulations*.

**Q: Do I need a permit to camp in a regular campground?**
A: No permit, but you need a site registration, and Fish Creek, Many Glacier, and St. Mary require reservations in peak season. See GLAC-FC-2025-01, *Frontcountry Campground Regulations*.

## 4. Campfires

**Q: Can I have a campfire at my campground?**
A: In frontcountry campgrounds, yes, in the designated fire ring at your site, between May 15 and September 30, unless the fire danger rating is Very High or Extreme or a temporary restriction is in effect. In the backcountry, it depends entirely on the specific campsite: some backcountry campgrounds allow fires in installed rings, while others prohibit wood fires year-round regardless of conditions. Check the designation for your exact campsite on the backcountry map when your permit is issued, and see GLAC-BC-2025-04, *Backcountry Camping Guide*, Section 4.

**Q: Can I bring my own firewood?**
A: Only firewood purchased within Flathead, Glacier, or Pondera counties may be brought into the Park, under the Montana firewood quarantine.

## 5. Wildlife

**Q: Do I need bear spray?**
A: It is not legally required, but the Park strongly recommends that every hiking party carry it and know how to use it. Rental and purchase locations are available in Apgar and St. Mary. See GLAC-WL-2026-02 for required food-storage practices, which are mandatory.

**Q: What do I do if I see a bear on the trail?**
A: Do not run. Group together, speak calmly, back away, and give the bear an escape route. Report any close encounter or charge to a ranger station within 24 hours.

## 6. Pets, Drones, and Miscellaneous

**Q: Can I bring my dog on trails?**
A: No. Pets are permitted only in developed areas, along roads, and in campgrounds, on a leash no longer than six feet. They are not permitted on any trail.

**Q: Are drones allowed?**
A: No. Launching, landing, or operating unmanned aircraft within the Park is prohibited.

**Q: Is there cell service?**
A: Coverage is minimal outside Apgar, West Glacier, and St. Mary. Do not rely on a phone for backcountry emergencies; the Park recommends carrying a satellite communicator.

**Q: Where can I fill water bottles?**
A: Potable water is available seasonally at visitor centers and most frontcountry campgrounds. All surface water in the backcountry must be filtered, boiled, or chemically treated.
