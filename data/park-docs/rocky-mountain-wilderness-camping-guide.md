# Rocky Mountain National Park — Wilderness Camping Guide

**Document No.:** ROMO-WC-2026-01
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Wilderness Office, Rocky Mountain National Park
**Supersedes:** ROMO-WC-2025-01

## 1. General Requirement

1.1 A wilderness camping permit is required for all overnight stays in the backcountry of Rocky Mountain National Park, year-round. Camping is permitted only at designated wilderness campsites, in designated cross-country zones, or at winter bivouac locations authorized under Section 7.

1.2 Permits specify sites and nights and must be carried by the trip leader. The permit itself does not exempt the holder's vehicle from the timed entry reservation system where in effect; see ROMO-FAQ-2026-01 for entry rules and the trailhead-parking exception.

1.3 Fire use at wilderness campsites is governed by ROMO-FIRE-2025-01, Section 4; the substantial majority of sites prohibit open fires entirely.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Designated site"** means a numbered wilderness campsite marked with a silver arrowhead post.
- **"Cross-country zone"** means one of the mapped zones where parties camp at undeveloped locations subject to Section 5.
- **"Stock site"** means a designated site equipped for horses and llamas.
- **"Technical bivouac"** means an overnight stay on a climbing route under Section 7.2.

## 3. Permits and Reservations

3.1 Reservations for the May 1 through October 31, 2026 season open on Recreation.gov at 8:00 a.m. Mountain Time on March 2, 2026, for the entire season. There is no lottery; inventory is continuous.

3.2 The administrative fee is $36 per trip, non-refundable. Winter permits (November 1 through April 30) are free and issued in person or by telephone.

3.3 Unreserved capacity is issued in person at the Beaver Meadows and Kawuneeche wilderness offices beginning the day before the trip start.

3.4 Maximum party size is 7 persons at standard designated sites and 12 at designated group sites. Cross-country zone parties may not exceed 7.

3.5 Maximum stay: 3 consecutive nights at any one site, 7 total wilderness nights June 1 through September 30, and 14 nights in the remainder of the year.

## 4. Conduct at Designated Sites

4.1 Tents must be pitched within the impacted core of the site, identified by the arrowhead post, and never on vegetation.

4.2 All food, trash, and scented items must be stored in a hard-sided bear-resistant container from May 1 through October 31 parkwide. Hanging is not an accepted method in this Park; trees at and above subalpine elevations do not reliably provide compliant geometry.

4.3 Water sources: maintain all camp activities, including stove operation and dishwashing, at least 100 feet from lakes and streams.

4.4 Privies exist at a minority of high-use sites (including Thunder Lake, Ouzel Lake, and Lawn Lake); elsewhere, bury human waste six inches deep 200 feet from water. Above treeline, all solid human waste must be packed out using a carry-out kit; burial is not permitted in alpine terrain.

## 5. Cross-Country Zones

5.1 Cross-country zone camping is intended for experienced parties. Camps must be at least 200 feet from water and trails, out of sight of other parties where terrain permits, below treeline unless the permit states otherwise, and moved at least one mile each night within the zone.

5.2 Open fires are prohibited in all cross-country zones without exception (see ROMO-FIRE-2025-01, Section 4.2).

## 6. Stock Use

6.1 Stock parties are limited to designated stock sites and to trails posted open to stock. Llamas are regulated as stock. Party limit is 8 head.

6.2 Certified weed-free feed is required within the Park; grazing is prohibited everywhere.

## 7. Winter and Technical Use

7.1 Winter camping (November 1 through April 30) is permitted in winter zones above designated snow depth, subject to the free winter permit. Avalanche terrain is not marked; parties are responsible for their own hazard evaluation.

7.2 Technical bivouacs on routes of Grade IV or longer (including the Diamond on Longs Peak) are authorized under a bivouac endorsement to the wilderness permit, limited to the face or ledge system named on the permit.

7.3 The Longs Peak Keyhole Route is non-technical for a limited season only, typically mid-July through early September; outside that window it is a mountaineering objective requiring technical equipment. Conditions reports are posted at the Longs Peak Ranger Station.

## 8. Wildlife

8.1 Black bears range throughout the Park below treeline; the container requirement in Section 4.2 is the primary mitigation. Moose frequent the Kawuneeche Valley and East Inlet drainages; maintain 25 yards from moose and elk and 75 yards during the autumn rut.

8.2 Mountain lion encounters are rare; solo trail running at dawn and dusk in the Wild Basin and North Inlet areas is discouraged.

## 9. Enforcement

Camping outside authorized locations, exceeding party size, storage violations, and fire violations are punishable under 36 CFR 1.6, 2.10, and 2.13. Wilderness rangers patrol throughout the season.

## 10. Contact

Wilderness Office: (970) 586-1242, daily 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Mountain Time in season. Site lists, the Appendix B fire-site list (maintained with ROMO-FIRE-2025-01), and current conditions are available at wilderness offices and on the Park website.
