# Great Smoky Mountains National Park — Frequently Asked Questions

**Document No.:** GRSM-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation, Great Smoky Mountains National Park

This document answers common visitor questions for 2026. It is informational; the cited regulations control where any conflict exists.

## 1. Entry, Parking, and Roads

**Q: Is there an entrance fee?**
A: No entrance fee is charged, a condition of the Park's founding deeds. However, a parking tag is required for any vehicle parked longer than 15 minutes anywhere in the Park: $5 daily, $15 weekly, or $40 annual, available online and at visitor centers. The tag is per vehicle, not per person, and does not guarantee a space.

**Q: Do I need a reservation to enter?**
A: No. The Park has no timed-entry system in 2026. Popular areas (Laurel Falls, Cades Cove, Clingmans Dome) fill early on peak days; arrive before 9:00 a.m. or after 3:00 p.m.

**Q: When is Cades Cove Loop Road vehicle-free?**
A: Every Wednesday from May 6 through September 30, 2026, the loop is closed to motor vehicles and open to bicycles and pedestrians all day.

**Q: Which roads close in winter?**
A: Clingmans Dome Road closes December 1 through March 31 annually. Parson Branch, Rich Mountain, Heintooga Ridge, and Balsam Mountain roads close seasonally as posted. Newfound Gap Road (US 441) remains open but closes temporarily for snow and ice at any time November through March; check the road status line at (865) 436-1200, option 2.

## 2. Camping

**Q: How do I camp in the backcountry?**
A: Reserve a specific numbered campsite or shelter up to 30 days ahead at smokiespermits.nps.gov ($8 per person per night, $40 trip cap). Camping is allowed only at designated sites; there is no dispersed camping. Party limit is 8 at most sites. See GRSM-BC-2025-01, *Backcountry Camping and Permit Guide*.

**Q: Can I just show up and camp?**
A: Not in the backcountry; every night requires a site-specific reservation, even in winter. Frontcountry campgrounds accept reservations on Recreation.gov, and some sites are same-day if unsold.

**Q: Are there bears?**
A: Approximately 1,900 black bears, one of the densest populations in the eastern United States. Food and scented items must be hung on the cables provided at every backcountry site or stored as directed in frontcountry campgrounds. Food storage violations carry fines and are the leading cause of campsite closures.

## 3. Campfires

**Q: Can I have a campfire while backpacking?**
A: Yes, at the established fire ring at your designated campsite or shelter, using dead and down wood gathered nearby, unless a burn ban is posted. Fires anywhere other than an established ring are always prohibited. Note that autumn is this Park's peak fire season, and Stage 1 bans (no backcountry fires) are common in October and November. See GRSM-FIRE-2025-02, Section 4.

**Q: Can I bring firewood into the Park?**
A: Only certified heat-treated firewood may cross the Park boundary. Untreated firewood is prohibited regardless of where it was cut. You may gather dead and down wood inside the Park for immediate use.

## 4. Trails

**Q: How do I hike to Clingmans Dome (Kuwohi)?**
A: The paved half-mile trail from the parking area climbs steeply to the observation tower. The parking area requires the standard parking tag. When Clingmans Dome Road is closed (December through March), the tower is reachable only by a 7-mile ski or hike from Newfound Gap.

**Q: Is Laurel Falls Trail open?**
A: The Laurel Falls corridor is operating under a pilot congestion plan in 2026; expect posted parking limits on weekends. Check current status at the Sugarlands Visitor Center.

**Q: Are dogs allowed on trails?**
A: No, with two exceptions: the Gatlinburg Trail and the Oconaluftee River Trail, on leash. Dogs are otherwise limited to campgrounds and road corridors.

## 5. Wildlife

**Q: What do I do if I see a bear?**
A: Keep at least 50 yards away; willfully approaching closer is illegal. If a bear approaches you, stand your ground, make noise, and appear large; do not run and do not drop food. Report aggressive bears to (865) 436-1230.

**Q: When can I see synchronous fireflies?**
A: The Elkmont firefly viewing event runs approximately eight nights in late May or early June (dates announced each April). Access is by lottery-issued vehicle pass only; the 2026 lottery ran April 24 through 27.

**Q: Are elk dangerous?**
A: Elk in the Cataloochee and Oconaluftee areas are wild and unpredictable, particularly during the autumn rut. Remain 50 yards away; parking on road shoulders to view elk is restricted as posted.

## 6. Miscellaneous

**Q: Is there cell service?**
A: Coverage is largely absent inside the Park. Download maps in advance. Emergency calls sometimes connect on ridge crests; Park dispatch is (865) 436-9171.

**Q: Can I fish?**
A: Yes, year-round, with a valid Tennessee or North Carolina license (either is valid parkwide). Brook trout possession rules and single-hook artificial-lure requirements apply; see the fishing regulations sheet at any visitor center.

**Q: Where are the visitor centers?**
A: Sugarlands (near Gatlinburg), Oconaluftee (near Cherokee), Cades Cove, and Clingmans Dome (seasonal).
