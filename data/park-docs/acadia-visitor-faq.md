# Acadia National Park — Frequently Asked Questions

**Document No.:** ACAD-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** April 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation and Education, Acadia National Park

This document answers common questions for the 2026 season. It is informational only; the regulations cited control where any conflict exists.

## 1. Entry and Cadillac Mountain

**Q: Do I need a reservation to enter Acadia?**
A: Not for the Park generally. A vehicle reservation is required only to drive the Cadillac Summit Road, from May 20 through October 22, 2026. A park entrance pass is required separately for all visitors. See ACAD-CL-2026-01, Section 3.1.

**Q: How do I get a Cadillac sunrise reservation?**
A: The same way as any Cadillac reservation: 30 percent are released 90 days ahead and 70 percent at 10:00 a.m. Eastern two days ahead, on Recreation.gov. Sunrise slots are the first to sell out, typically within minutes of the two-day release.

**Q: Can I hike or bike up Cadillac without a reservation?**
A: Yes. Reservations apply only to private vehicles on the Summit Road. The North Ridge and South Ridge trails and the road shoulder (for bicycles) require no reservation.

## 2. Camping

**Q: Can I backpack or camp in the backcountry?**
A: No. Acadia has no backcountry or dispersed camping anywhere, in any season. Camping is permitted only at the four designated campgrounds (Blackwoods, Seawall, Schoodic Woods, and Duck Harbor on Isle au Haut), all by reservation. See ACAD-CG-2025-02, Section 3.

**Q: Can I sleep in my car at a trailhead?**
A: No. Overnight vehicle occupancy outside a registered campsite is prohibited and enforced year-round.

## 3. Campfires

**Q: Where can I have a campfire?**
A: Only in the installed fireplaces at designated campground sites and at four designated picnic areas (Thompson Island, Pretty Marsh, Frazer Point, Seawall). Fires anywhere else — beaches, summits, and all undeveloped areas — are prohibited at all times. Duck Harbor campground allows no fires at all; stoves only. See ACAD-CG-2025-02, Section 4.

**Q: Can I bring firewood from home?**
A: Not from out of state. Maine's firewood quarantine prohibits importing untreated firewood; buy certified heat-treated or local wood. Gathering wood inside the Park is also prohibited.

## 4. Trails and Carriage Roads

**Q: Can I ride an e-bike on the carriage roads?**
A: Class 1 (pedal-assist) e-bikes only. Class 2 and 3 e-bikes are prohibited on carriage roads. All bicycles are prohibited on hiking trails and on the Land & Garden Preserve carriage roads where posted. See ACAD-CR-2025-05, Section 3.

**Q: Why is the Precipice Trail closed?**
A: Peregrine falcon nesting. The Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, and Valley Cove trails close each spring (March 15 in 2026) and reopen when chicks fledge, typically in early August. See ACAD-CL-2026-01, Section 4.

**Q: Are the ladder trails suitable for children or dogs?**
A: Ladder trails (Precipice, Beehive, and similar) involve exposed iron-rung climbing and are unsuitable for small children, and pets are prohibited on them. The Bowl via the Bowl Trail is the standard family alternative to the Beehive.

**Q: Can I bring my dog?**
A: Acadia is one of the more pet-friendly national parks: leashed pets (6-foot maximum) are allowed on most trails and carriage roads. Exceptions: ladder trails, Sand Beach and Echo Lake Beach in season, Duck Harbor Campground, and Wild Gardens of Acadia.

## 5. Tides and Coastal Safety

**Q: When can I walk the Bar Island land bridge?**
A: Approximately 1.5 hours on either side of low tide. Visitors are stranded on Bar Island regularly; the wait for the next low tide is about nine hours, and emergency boat retrieval is billed to the visitor. Consult a tide chart, not a fixed clock time.

**Q: Is it safe to climb on rocks at Thunder Hole?**
A: Stay behind railings during any surf event. Rogue waves have swept visitors from the lower viewing area; the gates close during storm swell.

## 6. Logistics

**Q: Is there a free shuttle?**
A: The fare-free Island Explorer bus operates June 23 through Columbus Day 2026, connecting campgrounds, trailheads, and Bar Harbor. Bicycles are carried on select routes. The Island Explorer does not serve the Cadillac Summit Road.

**Q: Where do I get drinking water?**
A: Campgrounds, the Hulls Cove Visitor Center, and Jordan Pond House in season. Carry water for all hikes; summit trails have none. Swimming and wading are prohibited in Jordan Pond, the public water supply.

**Q: What are the quiet hours at campgrounds?**
A: 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. See ACAD-CG-2025-02, Section 3.5.
