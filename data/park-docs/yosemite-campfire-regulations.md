# Yosemite National Park — Campfire and Stove Regulations

**Document No.:** YOSE-FIRE-2025-02
**Effective Date:** May 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Office of the Superintendent, Yosemite National Park, El Portal, CA
**Supersedes:** YOSE-FIRE-2023-01

## 1. Purpose

These regulations govern the use of campfires, charcoal fires, and portable stoves throughout Yosemite National Park. They implement 36 CFR 2.13 and the Superintendent's Compendium. Wilderness camping conduct generally is addressed in YOSE-WILD-2026-01, *Wilderness Permit and Trip Planning Guide*; this document is the controlling authority on fire.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Campfire"** means any open wood or charcoal fire.
- **"Established fire ring"** means a pre-existing rock fire ring in wilderness, or an NPS-installed fire ring or grate in a developed campground.
- **"Portable stove"** means a manufactured appliance burning pressurized gas, liquid fuel, or solid fuel tablets, capable of being switched off.
- **"Valley floor"** means the developed area of Yosemite Valley below the 4,600-foot contour.

## 3. Developed Campgrounds and Picnic Areas

3.1 Campfires in developed campgrounds are permitted only in NPS-installed fire rings and grates.

3.2 On the Valley floor, campfires (including charcoal) are permitted only between 5:00 p.m. and 10:00 p.m. from May 1 through September 30, in order to limit evening smoke accumulation under inversion conditions. Outside those hours during that period, no new fuel may be added and fires must be extinguished. From October 1 through April 30, Valley floor campfires are permitted at any hour, conditions permitting.

3.3 Outside Yosemite Valley, campground campfires are permitted at any hour unless a restriction under Section 6 is in effect.

3.4 Only dead and down wood may be gathered, and only outside Yosemite Valley and outside sequoia groves; wood gathering is prohibited on the Valley floor and above 9,600 feet. Imported firewood must comply with California's invasive-pest advisories.

## 4. Wilderness Fires

4.1 In wilderness areas of the Park, campfires are permitted only in established fire rings, and only at elevations below 9,600 feet. Campfires are prohibited above 9,600 feet throughout the Park, year-round, due to slow-growing subalpine fuels. This elevation prohibition does not vary with fire danger and is not subject to waiver.

4.2 Certain drainages below 9,600 feet are additionally closed to fires as posted, including within 0.25 miles of Laurel Lake outflow and in the Merced Lake shoreline zone; consult the wilderness trailhead postings and YOSE-WILD-2026-01, Appendix B.

4.3 Construction of new fire rings is prohibited. Where an established ring exists, it must be used as found; enlarging a ring is prohibited.

4.4 Wilderness fires must be attended at all times, kept smaller than three feet in diameter, and extinguished with water until cold to the touch. Burying a fire does not satisfy this requirement.

## 5. Stoves

5.1 Portable stoves are permitted throughout the Park, including above 9,600 feet, except during a Stage 3 restriction under Section 6.

5.2 A California Campfire Permit is required to operate a stove or fire in wilderness; the permit is issued automatically with a Yosemite wilderness permit and is otherwise available free online.

5.3 Twig-burning stoves and other appliances that burn unpressurized solid biomass are regulated as campfires, not as stoves, and are therefore prohibited above 9,600 feet and wherever campfires are restricted.

## 6. Temporary Fire Restrictions

6.1 The Superintendent may impose staged restrictions based on fuel moisture, weather, and fire activity:

- **Stage 1:** Wilderness campfires prohibited parkwide; campground fires permitted.
- **Stage 2:** All campfires prohibited parkwide, including developed campgrounds; portable gas stoves permitted.
- **Stage 3:** All ignition sources prohibited except pressurized-gas stoves in developed campgrounds only.

6.2 Restrictions are posted at entrance stations, wilderness centers, and on the Park website, and take precedence over Sections 3 and 4 wherever more restrictive.

6.3 Smoking is prohibited whenever Stage 1 or higher is in effect, except within an enclosed vehicle or building, or in a developed area cleared of flammable material.

## 7. Prohibited Acts

7.1 The following are prohibited at all times, parkwide: fires outside established rings; fires left unattended; burning of garbage, food waste, or non-wood materials; fires within sequoia groves; and the use of accelerants other than commercial fire starters.

7.2 Violation of this document is punishable under 36 CFR 1.6 and 2.13 by a fine of up to $5,000, imprisonment up to six months, or both, and by liability for suppression costs where a violation results in a wildfire.
