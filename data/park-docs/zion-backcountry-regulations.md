# Zion National Park — Backcountry and Wilderness Regulations

**Document No.:** ZION-BC-2025-03
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2025
**Issuing Office:** Wilderness and Backcountry Office, Zion National Park, Springdale, UT
**Supersedes:** ZION-BC-2022-01

## 1. Scope

These regulations govern overnight and technical day use of the backcountry and designated wilderness of Zion National Park, including designated backcountry campsites, at-large camping zones, and technical canyoneering routes. Permit reservation procedures for the Virgin River Narrows are detailed separately in ZION-NAR-2026-01, *Narrows Trip Planning and Permit Guide*. Frontcountry campgrounds (Watchman, South, Lava Point) are governed by posted campground rules and are outside the scope of this document.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Backcountry"** means all Park lands more than 100 feet from a paved road or developed area, excluding frontcountry campgrounds.
- **"Designated site"** means a numbered campsite along the West Rim, La Verkin Creek, Hop Valley, or Narrows corridors.
- **"At-large zone"** means an area where camping is permitted at any compliant location, as mapped in the current Wilderness Guide.
- **"Technical route"** means any route requiring rappelling or other rope work, including all slot canyons subject to permit.
- **"Stove"** means a manufactured appliance burning pressurized gas or liquid fuel that can be switched off.

## 3. Permits

3.1 A wilderness permit is required for: all overnight backcountry stays; all technical routes; and all through-hikes of the Virgin River Narrows from Chamberlain's Ranch.

3.2 Advance reservations are available on the Park's permit system up to two months ahead (released on the 5th of each month at 10:00 a.m. Mountain Time); last-minute drawing requests are accepted 2 to 7 days before the trip date; remaining spaces are issued in person the day before or day of the trip at the Zion Canyon Wilderness Desk.

3.3 Maximum group size is 12 persons for most areas, and 6 persons in designated slot canyons and the Subway (Left Fork) corridor. Groups may not split and recombine to evade these limits.

3.4 Permits must be carried and produced on request. Itineraries are fixed as to site and night for designated sites.

## 4. Fires

4.1 Wood and charcoal fires, including twig stoves and any device burning unpressurized biomass, are prohibited throughout the backcountry of Zion National Park at all times, in all seasons, at all elevations, and under all fire-danger conditions. There is no season, site, or condition under which a backcountry campfire is authorized in this Park.

4.2 Pressurized-gas and liquid-fuel stoves are permitted in the backcountry, except during a total fire ban declared under the Superintendent's Compendium, during which the Compendium's terms control.

4.3 Wood fires in frontcountry campgrounds are permitted only in installed fire rings and grills at Watchman and South campgrounds, subject to posted seasonal restrictions; this allowance does not extend to Lava Point, where fires are prohibited when posted during dry periods.

4.4 Possession of firewood in the backcountry is prohibited.

## 5. Camping Conduct

5.1 In at-large zones, camps must be located out of sight of any trail, at least 200 feet from any water source or archaeological site, and at least one mile from any road or trailhead unless the zone map states otherwise.

5.2 Camping is prohibited: within Zion Canyon between the Temple of Sinawava and the South Campground; on the West Rim Trail outside designated sites; within 100 feet of any spring; and anywhere within the Left Fork (Subway) corridor.

5.3 Maximum stay is three consecutive nights at any designated site or within any single at-large zone, and 14 backcountry nights per calendar year per person.

5.4 Quiet hours (10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m.) apply at all designated sites.

## 6. Water, Sanitation, and Waste

6.1 All human waste in the Narrows corridor, the Left Fork corridor, and all slot canyons must be packed out using a commercial waste containment system; disposal kits are available at the Wilderness Desk. Elsewhere, waste must be buried six inches deep at least 200 feet from water.

6.2 All trash and toilet paper must be packed out parkwide. Burying or burning refuse is prohibited (see Section 4.1).

6.3 Springs are the sole reliable water sources on plateau routes; consult the Wilderness Desk for current flow status. All water must be treated. During cyanobacteria advisories on the Virgin River, filtering does not render the water safe; carry all water for Narrows trips when an advisory is posted.

## 7. Hazards Specific to This Park

7.1 Flash floods are the principal hazard in all canyon routes. Permits for the Narrows and technical canyons are suspended automatically when the National Weather Service issues a flash flood warning for the area, and may be suspended under a flash flood watch at the discretion of the Wilderness Desk. See ZION-NAR-2026-01, Section 6.

7.2 Summer plateau and canyon temperatures regularly exceed 100 °F; each hiker on exposed routes should carry a minimum of one gallon of water per day.

7.3 Cliff edges on the West Rim and Angels Landing routes are unfenced. Angels Landing beyond Scout Lookout requires a separate day-use permit issued by seasonal lottery.

## 8. Enforcement

Violations of these regulations, including the fire prohibition in Section 4.1, are punishable under 36 CFR 1.6, 2.10, and 2.13. Rangers patrol backcountry corridors regularly, and fire scars are investigated as resource damage with restitution assessed.

## 9. Contact

Zion Canyon Wilderness Desk: (435) 772-0170. Current advisories, water source status, and same-day permit availability are posted at the desk each morning. See ZION-FAQ-2026-01 for plain-language guidance.
