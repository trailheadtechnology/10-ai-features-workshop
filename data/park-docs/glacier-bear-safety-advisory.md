# Glacier National Park — Bear Safety Advisory and Required Practices

**Document No.:** GLAC-WL-2026-02
**Effective Date:** April 1, 2026 (last revised June 26, 2026)
**Issuing Office:** Science and Resources Management, Wildlife Branch, Glacier National Park

## 1. Purpose

This advisory sets out required and recommended practices for travel in grizzly and black bear habitat within Glacier National Park, and lists current elevated-activity notices. All areas of the Park are bear habitat; the absence of a notice for a particular area does not indicate the absence of bears.

Requirements in Sections 3 and 4 are enforceable under 36 CFR 2.10 and 36 CFR 2.2 and under the Superintendent's Compendium. Area restrictions arising from bear activity are also published in GLAC-CL-2026-01, *Seasonal Closures and Trail Status*.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Approved container"** means a bear-resistant food container listed by the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee as of the effective date of this advisory.
- **"Attractant"** means any food, beverage, garbage, cookware, toiletry, or other scented item capable of drawing wildlife.
- **"Displacement behavior"** means a bear abandoning natural foraging in response to human presence, as determined by Wildlife Branch staff.
- **"Elevated-activity notice"** means a formal notice under Section 5 of this advisory.

## 3. Required Practices — All Visitors

3.1 All attractants must be secured at all times when not in immediate use: in frontcountry areas, in a hard-sided vehicle or Park food locker (see GLAC-FC-2025-01, Section 5); in the backcountry, in an approved container or on a Park-installed hanging system (see GLAC-BC-2025-04, Section 3.5).

3.2 Feeding, baiting, or intentionally approaching within 100 yards of a bear is prohibited.

3.3 Visitors must report all bear encounters involving charging, contact, or loss of food to any ranger station within 24 hours.

3.4 Dragging, burying, or abandoning fish entrails within 200 feet of any trail, campsite, or shoreline is prohibited; entrails must be punctured and deposited in deep water.

## 4. Recommended Practices — Trail Travel

4.1 Hike in groups of three or more. Since 1980, no fatal bear attack in the Park has involved a group of four or more traveling together and making noise.

4.2 Carry bear spray in an immediately accessible holster, not inside a pack. Know its range (approximately 25 feet) and expiration date. Bear spray may not be carried on Park shuttles' exterior racks and may not be flown in carry-on or checked baggage.

4.3 Make noise, particularly near running water, in dense vegetation, and on blind corners. Bells alone are not considered adequate.

4.4 If you encounter a grizzly at close range and it has not charged, group together, speak calmly, and back away slowly. Do not run. If contact is imminent following a charge, use bear spray; if contact occurs, lie face down, protect the neck, and remain still.

## 5. Elevated-Activity Notices in Effect

5.1 **Hidden Meadow Trail corridor — notice issued June 26, 2026.** Beginning in late June 2026, Wildlife Branch staff received multiple credible reports of a grizzly sow with two cubs foraging within 100 meters of the Hidden Meadow Trail, including two incidents of displacement behavior directed at hikers on June 22 and June 25, 2026. Effective June 26, 2026, travel on the Hidden Meadow Trail is permitted only in groups of four or more persons, off-trail travel within one mile of Hidden Meadow is prohibited, and trail runners are advised to select an alternate route. This notice parallels the restriction published as Section 4.2 of GLAC-CL-2026-01 and remains in effect until rescinded by revision of this advisory. If the sow's behavior escalates, the corridor will be closed to all entry without further notice.

5.2 **Many Glacier Valley — standing seasonal notice.** Huckleberry concentrations typically draw bears to slopes above the Grinnell Glacier and Iceberg Lake trails from late July through September. Groups of three or more recommended; expect short-notice trail closures.

5.3 **Camas Road corridor** — a food-conditioned black bear was relocated on June 8, 2026. No restriction is in effect, but roadside parking to view bears ("bear jams") is prohibited along the entire corridor.

## 6. Backcountry Campsite Conduct

6.1 Cook and eat only in the designated food-preparation area of each backcountry campground, never at the tent pads.

6.2 Sleep a minimum of 100 yards from the food-preparation area where site layout permits.

6.3 The odor profile of a campsite is a management concern independent of fire policy; note that at no-wood-fire sites such as the Sperry Chalet area, food waste must be packed out rather than burned, without exception (see GLAC-BC-2025-04, Section 4.2).

## 7. Reporting

Report bear sightings involving aggression, property damage, or food loss to (406) 888-7800, or in person at any visitor center or ranger station. Routine sightings may be reported at trailhead registers. Timely reports materially affect the Park's ability to issue notices under Section 5 before an incident occurs.
