# Zion National Park — Frequently Asked Questions

**Document No.:** ZION-FAQ-2026-01
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Division of Interpretation, Zion National Park

This document summarizes common visitor questions for 2026. It is informational; the referenced regulations control where any conflict exists.

## 1. Getting Around

**Q: Can I drive my car into Zion Canyon?**
A: Not during shuttle season (February 14 through November 29, 2026). The Scenic Drive is shuttle, bicycle, and pedestrian only during that period. The free Canyon Line shuttle runs from the visitor center starting at 6:00 a.m. In winter, private vehicles may use the Scenic Drive. See ZION-SH-2026-01, *Shuttle Operations and Canyon Access Notice*.

**Q: Do I need a reservation just to enter the Park?**
A: No. Zion does not use a parkwide entry reservation in 2026. Specific activities require permits: Angels Landing (beyond Scout Lookout), all overnight backcountry trips, top-down Narrows trips, and technical canyons.

**Q: My RV is 12 feet tall. Can I drive Route 9 through the Park?**
A: You will need a tunnel escort permit ($15) for the Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel if your vehicle is 7'10" wide or 11'4" tall or larger. Escorts run 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. daily.

## 2. Angels Landing and the Narrows

**Q: How do I get an Angels Landing permit?**
A: By seasonal lottery on Recreation.gov (applications roughly two months ahead) or the day-before lottery. The permit covers the section beyond Scout Lookout only; hiking to Scout Lookout requires no permit.

**Q: Do I need a permit to hike the Narrows?**
A: Bottom-up from the Temple of Sinawava as far as Big Spring: no permit. Top-down from Chamberlain's Ranch (day or overnight): permit required. Overnight sites sell out within about an hour of release on the 5th of the preceding month. See ZION-NAR-2026-01, *Narrows Trip Planning and Permit Guide*.

**Q: When does the Narrows close?**
A: Automatically whenever flow exceeds 150 CFS or the National Weather Service issues a flash flood warning. Spring snowmelt typically keeps flow above the threshold for several weeks in April and May. Check the gauge before traveling.

## 3. Camping and Fires

**Q: Can I have a campfire while backpacking in Zion?**
A: No. Wood and charcoal fires are prohibited everywhere in Zion's backcountry, in every season, with no exceptions for fire rings, low fire danger, or winter. Only pressurized-fuel stoves are allowed, and even stoves may be suspended during a total fire ban. See ZION-BC-2025-03, Section 4.

**Q: What about at the campgrounds?**
A: Wood fires are allowed in the installed rings at Watchman and South campgrounds unless a posted restriction is in effect. Lava Point may be posted no-fire during dry periods.

**Q: Where can I camp for free outside a campground?**
A: Nowhere inside the Park without a wilderness permit, and permits carry fees. Dispersed camping is available on adjacent Bureau of Land Management lands outside Park boundaries, subject to BLM rules.

## 4. Safety

**Q: How much water should I carry?**
A: On exposed summer routes, one gallon per person per day at minimum. Summer canyon temperatures regularly exceed 100 °F, and most heat casualties occur on the return leg of short, popular trails.

**Q: What is the cyanobacteria advisory?**
A: Algal mats in the Virgin River system periodically produce toxins that filtering does not remove. While an advisory is posted, do not drink filtered river water; carry all drinking water and avoid submerging your head. Status is posted at the visitor center.

**Q: Is Angels Landing dangerous?**
A: The route beyond Scout Lookout follows an exposed fin with chain assistance and unfenced drops exceeding 1,000 feet. Do not attempt it in wet, icy, or high-wind conditions, and do not take children who cannot maintain three points of contact unassisted.

## 5. Wildlife and Pets

**Q: Can I bring my dog?**
A: Pets are permitted only on the Pa'rus Trail (leashed, 6-foot maximum) and in developed areas and campgrounds. They are prohibited on all other trails, in the backcountry, and on shuttles.

**Q: Are there dangerous animals?**
A: Zion supports mountain lions, rattlesnakes, and, at higher elevations, black bears (rare). The most frequent wildlife incidents involve rock squirrels biting visitors who feed them, which is prohibited and subject to fine.

## 6. Seasons

**Q: What is the best time to visit?**
A: April, May, September, and October offer moderate temperatures and full shuttle service. Summer brings extreme heat and monsoon flash-flood risk in the afternoons. Winter offers private-vehicle access to the canyon and light crowds; high-elevation trails hold ice, and traction devices are advised.

**Q: Is anything closed in 2026 I should know about?**
A: Closures change frequently with flow, storms, and rockfall; the visitor center posts current conditions each morning. Standing restrictions, including the private-land segment at the top of the Narrows and the prohibition on camping in the Left Fork corridor, appear in ZION-BC-2025-03, Section 5.
