# Zion National Park — Narrows Trip Planning and Permit Guide

**Document No.:** ZION-NAR-2026-01
**Effective Date:** February 1, 2026
**Issuing Office:** Wilderness and Backcountry Office, Zion National Park

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This guide describes permit requirements, trip options, and hazard management for travel in the Virgin River Narrows (the "Narrows"). It supplements ZION-BC-2025-03, *Backcountry and Wilderness Regulations*, which remains controlling on camping conduct, fires, and sanitation. Nothing in this guide authorizes travel during a permit suspension under Section 6.

## 2. Definitions

- **"Bottom-up day use"** means travel upstream from the Temple of Sinawava, no farther than Big Spring, returning the same day.
- **"Top-down through-hike"** means the 16-mile route from Chamberlain's Ranch to the Temple of Sinawava, in one or two days.
- **"Narrows campsite"** means one of the 12 numbered sites between Big Spring and the Chamberlain's Ranch trail segment.
- **"Flow rate"** means the discharge of the North Fork of the Virgin River in cubic feet per second (CFS) as measured at the USGS gauge near Springdale.

## 3. Permit Requirements

3.1 Bottom-up day use to Big Spring requires no permit. Travel upstream of Big Spring without a top-down permit is prohibited.

3.2 All top-down through-hikes require a wilderness permit, whether completed in one day or with an overnight at a Narrows campsite.

3.3 Overnight through-hikes are limited to one night, at the specific numbered campsite on the permit. Party size at Narrows campsites is limited to 6 persons at sites 1 through 9 and 12 persons at sites 10 through 12.

3.4 Permits are issued under the calendar and drawing system described in ZION-BC-2025-03, Section 3.2. Overnight Narrows sites for the peak season (April through October 2026) are released on the 5th of the preceding month and are typically exhausted within one hour of release.

## 4. Access

4.1 Chamberlain's Ranch is reached by an 18-mile dirt road outside the Park boundary, impassable when wet. No shuttle service is provided by the Park; commercial shuttles operate from Springdale under permit.

4.2 The exit is via the Riverside Walk to the Temple of Sinawava shuttle stop. During shuttle season (February 14 through November 29, 2026), private vehicles may not be left at the Temple of Sinawava; through-hikers must ride the canyon shuttle. See ZION-SH-2026-01, *Shuttle Operations and Canyon Access Notice*.

4.3 The upper river crosses private land for the first three miles; camping, fires, and off-route travel are prohibited on this segment (fires are in any case prohibited throughout the route; see ZION-BC-2025-03, Section 4.1).

## 5. Required and Recommended Equipment

5.1 Required for all top-down trips: footwear suitable for sustained in-water travel (open sandals are considered inadequate and issuance may be refused), and one waste containment kit per party per day (Section 7).

5.2 Recommended: wooden or trekking staff, dry bags for all gear, and, at water temperatures below 55 °F, wetsuits or drysuits. Water temperature and flow are posted daily at the Wilderness Desk.

5.3 From November through April, drysuit-level protection is strongly recommended; hypothermia is the leading cause of Narrows rescues outside flood events.

## 6. Flow and Weather Suspensions

6.1 Permits are not issued, and existing permits are suspended, when the flow rate exceeds 150 CFS. Between 120 and 150 CFS, permits are issued with a mandatory hazard briefing.

6.2 All Narrows travel, including bottom-up day use, is closed automatically upon issuance of a flash flood warning by the National Weather Service for the North Fork drainage. The Wilderness Desk may also suspend permits under a flash flood watch or when monsoon forecasting indicates significant risk.

6.3 Suspensions under this section are not eligible for fee refunds; permits may be rescheduled once, subject to availability, within the same season.

6.4 The Narrows drains a watershed extending 20 miles beyond the Park; storms not visible from within the canyon can produce flood pulses. Rising water, debris, or a sudden change in water color requires immediate movement to high ground.

## 7. Sanitation

7.1 All solid human waste generated between Chamberlain's Ranch and the Temple of Sinawava must be packed out in a containment system. There are no toilets between the trailhead and Riverside Walk. Kits are distributed with each permit at no additional charge.

7.2 Urination directly into the flowing river channel is the accepted practice within the Narrows, contrary to general backcountry guidance, due to the absence of suitable soil.

## 8. Cyanobacteria Advisory

8.1 Benign-appearing algal mats in the North Fork have periodically produced cyanotoxins. While an advisory is in effect, do not filter and drink river water; carry all drinking water, and prevent submersion of the head where practicable. Advisory status is posted at the Wilderness Desk and in ZION-FAQ-2026-01.

## 9. Enforcement

Travel above Big Spring without a permit, camping at other than the assigned site, or failure to carry required equipment is punishable under 36 CFR 1.6. Violations recorded during flood suspensions are treated as aggravated due to rescue exposure.

## 10. Contact

Wilderness Desk: (435) 772-0170. Flow rate is available from the USGS gauge (09405500) and is posted daily at the visitor center and Wilderness Desk.
