# The decision framework

This is the document the whole day builds toward. Each feature closes with the same three-part card: when to reach for the feature, what it roughly costs, and the one-liner for your CTO. Those cards accumulate here, so by the closing session this page is a complete answer to the question you brought to the workshop: which of these problems do my users have, and what would it cost to solve them?

How to use it back at the office: go down the "when to reach for this" column and mark every row that describes a problem your users actually have. Sort your marks by the cost column, cheapest first. The top of that list is your first AI feature, and the one-liner column is how you pitch it.

This table is a working draft. The one-liners get refined as each feature's demo and lab are built, and the cost estimates get sharpened against the real implementations.

| # | Feature | When to reach for this | Rough cost & effort | The one-liner for your CTO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Summarization](../modules/1-understanding/01-summarization/) | Users face long content they don't want to read: reviews, tickets, reports, meeting notes, threads | Days. One API call per document, free local models, no new infrastructure | "Our users are drowning in text we already have. A weekend of prompt work turns it into answers." |
| 2 | [Extraction](../modules/1-understanding/02-extraction/) | Valuable data is trapped in documents: invoices, resumes, emails, contracts, legacy records | Days to a working pipeline. The real work is schema design and accuracy spot-checks | "We have years of data trapped in documents. This turns it into queryable rows without anyone re-typing it." |
| 3 | [Sentiment](../modules/1-understanding/03-sentiment/) | A high-volume text stream needs a judgment call at scale: reviews, NPS, tickets, mentions | Days. The diligence is a labeled sample and an error count; local models make unit cost near zero | "We measured: the free local model matches the expensive one on most of this task, and we know exactly which slice needs the big gun." |
| 4 | [Semantic search](../modules/2-finding/04-semantic-search/) | Users complain about search, or describe what they want instead of naming it | Days to a prototype. Embeddings are cheap to free; the work is evaluation and tuning | "Our search finds what users mean, not just what they type." |
| 5 | [RAG](../modules/2-finding/05-rag/) | The org already wrote the answers down and people still ask humans: knowledge bases, policies, docs | Weeks to production quality. Chunking, retrieval evaluation, and document freshness are the real work | "Our documents already answer these questions. This makes them answer directly, with receipts." |
| 6 | [Recommendations](../modules/2-finding/06-recommendations/) | Users finish one thing in your catalog and get no next step | Nearly free on top of semantic search, days from scratch. Behavior-based methods can come later | "The same vectors that power our search give us 'more like this' for free." |
| 7 | [Classification & routing](../modules/3-deciding/07-classification-routing/) | A human sorts a shared inbox or queue before anyone acts | Days, no training data needed to start. Ongoing taxonomy refinement as traffic reveals edge cases | "Every message reaches the right person in seconds, and the urgent ones stop waiting in line." |
| 8 | [Anomaly detection](../modules/3-deciding/08-anomaly-detection/) | A stream of routine text where the rare exception is expensive to miss: tickets, logs, safety reports | Days, cheap to run forever. Embeddings plus arithmetic your team already knows | "We hear about the washed-out bridge from the first three reports, not from a one-star review a month later." |
| 9 | [Human-in-the-loop](../modules/3-deciding/09-human-in-the-loop/) | Any customer-facing or high-stakes generation sent under your organization's name | Weeks of ordinary product engineering: approval UI, audit trail, policy design. Often what makes the other features shippable | "The AI does the typing, our people keep the judgment, and nothing goes out without a human OK until the data says it can." |
| 10 | [Agentic workflows](../modules/4-doing/10-agentic-workflows/) | Multi-step, goal-shaped workflows spanning systems, where users currently do the clicking | The highest of the ten: weeks to months, frontier-model costs, real guardrail and observability work | "Users state the goal in one sentence, the software does the clicking, and a human still signs off on anything that matters." |

Two cross-cutting lessons from the day belong in any pitch built from this table. First, rows 1 through 8 mostly run on free local models, so the cost conversation is smaller than leadership expects. Second, row 9 is the trust pattern that de-risks everything else: leading your pitch with the human-in-the-loop design is usually what gets the other rows approved.
